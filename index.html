About

Code that survives contact with real users.

I started building with Flutter in 2023 and haven't stopped — three years and a small stack of shipped apps later, I still think it's the best tool available for building genuinely native-feeling mobile experiences from a single codebase.

My focus has narrowed over time to a specific kind of problem: apps that need to work when conditions aren't ideal — spotty connectivity, low-end devices, real concurrent users. That's pushed me deep into offline-first architecture, careful state management with Riverpod and BLoC, and treating the data layer as seriously as the UI.

I'm currently a Computer Science undergraduate, which keeps the fundamentals sharp, and I split my time between freelance client work and personal projects built specifically to get better at the parts of engineering that don't show up in a tutorial — sync conflict resolution, animation performance, and code that another developer can actually maintain.
